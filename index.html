Course Overview
AP Business with Personal Finance — College-level introductory business & personal finance
AP Business with Personal Finance is a one-semester, college-level equivalent course covering entrepreneurship, marketing, finance, accounting, and management — plus personal finance. Units 1–4 are assessed on the AP Exam. Unit 5 (personal finance) may be taught before or after the exam depending on your school's calendar.

Unit 1: Businesses, Competition, and New Ideas

~37 class periods · 20–30% AP Exam weighting · ~12–14 days for Business Canvas Project

Exam-assessed

Developing understanding

From a small neighborhood shop to a large global corporation, all businesses share key characteristics: They start as an idea and seek to achieve viability by selling products that address customers' problems, needs, or wants. Unit 1 introduces students to the world of business — how businesses get started, how they're organized, how they compete, how ethics shape decision making, and how external factors affect success. Students also consider how these same forces influence individuals' career opportunities, income, and personal choices about work.

Business Canvas Project tasks (Unit 1)

2

3

4

AP Exam preparation tip

Unit 1 introduces core terminology — value, competitive advantage, differentiation, risk — that recurs throughout the exam. Questions ask students to describe factors affecting businesses and individuals and explain how and why businesses pursue specific goals and strategies.

Unit 2: Marketing

~34 class periods · 20–30% AP Exam weighting · ~5–6 days for Business Canvas Project

Exam-assessed

Developing understanding

What separates a successful product launch from a failure? Unit 2 introduces students to the critical role marketing plays in a business's success. Students explore how businesses identify target customers, research markets, develop products, set prices, select distribution channels, and craft promotional strategies — all with the goal of satisfying customer needs profitably.

Unit 5: Personal Goals, Budgeting, and Investing

~24 class periods (+20 optional for additional PF coverage) · Financial Advisor Project

Not on AP Exam
Unit 5 is designed to be taught either before or after the AP Exam, as appropriate to a school's calendar. It is not assessed on the multiple-choice section of the exam.

Developing understanding

In Unit 5, students connect all the personal finance concepts learned throughout the course — PESTEL and career considerations (U1), consumer decision making (U2), saving/borrowing/investing (U3), earning income (U4) — and explore in greater depth topics such as taxes, risk, and insurance. Students practice interpreting a pay stub, planning a household budget, and analyzing how risk tolerance, time horizon, and spending habits impact long-term financial security.

Course Skills
All five skill categories with sub-skills verbatim from the CED. Skills are assessed across all exam question types.
Students should develop all five skill categories throughout the course. Skills are assessed via multiple-choice and free-response questions on the AP Exam.
Business skills

Skill Category 1 — Concept Application

Explain business and personal finance concepts, principles, and strategies, and apply them to scenarios.
1.ADescribe business and personal finance concepts, principles, and strategies.
1.BInterpret quantitative and qualitative business and personal financial data, performing calculations as appropriate.
1.CUsing business and personal finance concepts and principles, explain how and why businesses and individuals pursue specific goals, strategies, and actions.

Skill Category 2 — Entrepreneurship

Identify a market opportunity, and develop and test a product idea designed to address the opportunity.
2.AIdentify a market opportunity (e.g., a customer problem, need, or want) and develop a product idea to address it.
2.BFormulate and test business hypotheses to iterate and improve on a product idea.
2.CExplain the desirability, viability, and/or feasibility of a product idea.
Professional & leadership skills

Skill Category 3 — Decision Making

Describe opportunities or problems, and recommend courses of action to address them.
3.ADescribe internal, market, and external factors that affect a business or individual, and explain how and why they create opportunities and/or problems.
3.BExplain how potential courses of action could capitalize on an opportunity or solve a problem for a business or individual.
3.CEstablish decision-making criteria and use them to systematically evaluate different courses of action a business or individual may take.
3.DRecommend a decisive course of action for a business or individual, and support the recommendation with persuasive reasoning and evidence.

Skill Category 4 — Communication

Create authentic communications appropriate for a specific audience and purpose.
4.APresent business and personal financial data (e.g., data visualizations and financial statements) in accurate, precise, and accessible formats targeted for a specific audience and purpose.
4.BCreate authentic business communications (e.g., surveys, business canvases, and pitches) that are targeted for a specific audience and purpose.

Skill Category 5 — Collaboration

Work collaboratively with and lead others to accomplish a goal or task.
5.ADevelop clear, shared team objectives related to a business task or project consistent with a vision, mission, and/or goals.
5.BDefine clear roles and responsibilities for members of a team working to accomplish a business task or project.
5.CDevelop and implement effective strategies to motivate individuals and teams to achieve goals related to a business task or project.
5.DFollow through on agreed-upon deliverables as a part of a team working to accomplish a business task or project.
Projects
The two required through-course projects: Business Canvas Project (Units 1–4) and Financial Advisor Project (Unit 5).
The Business Canvas Project runs through Units 1–4. Students act as entrepreneurs developing a business idea from initial concept through strategic planning. Dedicated class time is required in each unit. Question 1 on the AP Exam (15% weight) is an exam-day validation of students' own Business Canvas Project.
The 8 canvas elements

Customer

Identification and validation of a customer problem, need, or want. Who does the business serve?

Product

The solution developed to address the customer's need. Includes the minimum viable product (MVP).

Market

The competitive landscape, target market size, and KPIs for market-related goals.

Operations

Production process, supply chain, and day-to-day running of the business.

Organization

Business structure, roles and responsibilities, vision and mission statements.

Finance / Accounting

Startup costs, sources of capital, projected income statement, and financial benchmarks.

Value Proposition

The core competencies and unique value the business delivers to customers.

Revenue

How the business generates income; pricing strategy and revenue model.

The Financial Advisor Project is a required culminating project in Unit 5. Students receive a fictional household financial profile and develop a comprehensive financial plan. Minimum 4 weeks of dedicated class time required (CR11). Not assessed on the AP Exam.
Comprehensive financial plan — 10 components

A concise overview of the household's situation, goals, and the advisor's key recommendations. Skills: 1.C, 4.B

Household overview (members, employment, income), short- and long-term financial goals, values and priorities, spending/savings habits, charitable giving, risk tolerance rating and rationale. Skills: 1.A, 4.B

Monthly net income, fixed/variable expenses, charitable contributions, savings, surplus/shortage; spending evaluation vs. guidelines; debt overview (balances, rates, payback periods); savings/investment account summary; net worth statement. Skills: 1.B, 1.C, 3.A, 4.B

Current insurance coverage review; adequacy assessment (adequate / insufficient / missing); insurance recommendations with budget impact; fraud and consumer protection recommendation. Skills: 3.A, 3.B, 3.D

Education goals overview; estimated cost and time horizon; potential funding sources; recommended monthly savings amount; recommended saving/investment vehicle (e.g., 529 plan, HYSA). Skills: 3.A, 3.B, 3.D

Housing goal overview; estimated home cost and affordability analysis; comparison to current costs; upfront costs (down payment, moving); recommendation to pursue or defer, supported by at least two numerical comparisons. Skills: 3.A, 3.B, 3.D

Retirement goal overview; savings estimates at 2%, 5%, 8%, and 10% return scenarios; recommended contribution strategy and vehicle; Social Security claiming age recommendation; charitable giving recommendation aligned to household values and budget. Skills: 3.A, 3.B, 3.D

Budget overview; monthly budget summary (income, all expense categories, insurance, charitable giving, education and retirement savings contributions, debt payments, surplus/shortage); explanation of notable changes; summary of how budget supports long-term goals. Skill: 4.A

Status quo repayment summary; recommended debt strategy (avalanche, snowball, or consolidation) with rationale and estimated monthly impact; projected benefits; impact on financial stability and progress toward long-term goals, noting trade-offs. Skill: 3.D

3–5 minute oral presentation or recorded pitch summarizing the comprehensive financial plan. Deliverable: oral presentation or recorded pitch (if required by teacher). Skill: 4.B
